# Manuscript Excerpt

This paper studies whether digital platform participation changes the growth path of small manufacturing firms. We focus on a mechanism that is central in search and matching models: platforms may reduce market frictions by allowing small firms to reach buyers beyond their local networks. If this mechanism is valid, platform participation should be associated not only with higher sales but also with broader customer geography and lower customer acquisition costs.

The empirical analysis uses firm-level panel data and compares firms before and after platform entry. The contribution is not simply to show that platforms correlate with performance. Instead, the paper aims to identify demand access and search-cost channels that explain why platform participation matters for small firms.
